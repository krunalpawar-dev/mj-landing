Better inventory starts with traceable movements

How warehouse workflows, barcodes and cycle counts support a more reliable view of stock.

MJ DYNAMIC·September 9, 2026·3 min read

Record the movement, not just the balance

A stock balance says how much should be available; a movement history helps explain why. Receiving, putaway, transfers, retrieval and adjustments should follow defined rules. Record the item, quantity, source, destination and responsible operation as appropriate so discrepancies can be investigated.

Match the physical workflow

Software steps should reflect how warehouse staff receive, locate and move goods. Barcode integration is useful when identifiers and scanning practices are consistent. Before implementation, check the location model, label conventions and common exceptions with the people doing the work.

Use cycle counts to investigate differences

Cycle counting helps compare recorded inventory with physical stock in smaller, manageable groups. Define how discrepancies are reviewed and who can approve an adjustment. A count is most useful when the team can trace the transactions that explain a difference and improve the underlying process.

Coordinate connected systems

ERP and transport integrations need clear ownership of records and events. Decide when a movement is confirmed, how failures are retried and how repeated messages are recognized. Inventory accuracy depends on disciplined workflows and reliable integration behavior; software alone does not guarantee a particular accuracy rate.
